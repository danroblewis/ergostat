# ergostat, reviewed again by a control engineer, now briefed

Reviewer: a control engineer who reads block diagrams, transfer functions, cascades and summing junctions every day. Before this pass I read my blind review (`reviews/control-engineer-blind.md`), the owner's intent (`intent.md`), `docs/WHY.md`, `docs/DISCIPLINES.md`, `docs/vocabulary.md`, `docs/proposals/diagram-options.md`, `docs/DECISIONS.md`, the diagram and web-page sections of `docs/DESIGN.md` and `README.md`, and every demo's README. I then walked the demos again at 1440x900, at 390x844, and in dark mode. All new screenshots are in `<workdir>/design/shots/` and start with `ceb-`. Screenshots from the blind pass start with `ce-`.

## What I got right and what I got wrong

My blind reading, at about 75% confidence, was: "a model of a working organisation as feedback loops, in two views, with identification, loop judging and gated actions, for an operator running an agent factory, with control engineers as a secondary audience."

- **Right:** it has one model and two views. The Dynamics view is "what is" and the Blocks view is "what is controlled". It identifies processes from the record and judges loops. People only approve or deny actions.
- **Wrong about the audience.** Engineers and SREs are a **primary** audience: "what they control and what they must do". The control view is meant to be read the way I read my own work, not watched over by a secondary checker. `DESIGN.md` §6 calls the control diagram "the product's main view", and `README.md` says the page "opens on the control diagram". That makes the problems with the Blocks view weigh more, not less.
- **Wrong about the scope.** I took the agent factory to be the product. It is only the demo. The product sits **on top of** existing monitoring, alerting and autoscalers and holds the model that relates them. Its promised value is explaining systems in design documents, projecting capacity and cost, predicting failures, and **"fixing the controls we already pay for"**, meaning autoscalers and alert rules identified as controllers. None of the demos shows that last use.
- **Wrong about `requests-handled` and `model-fits`.** I called these "the tool's own bookkeeping". They are deliberate: each demo models ergostat's own modelling work as a system (`modelling`) that keeps the model honest. The idea is sound. The way it is shown is not (finding 6).

## 1. Where the application failed to tell me what it is for

1. **The first screen is a system dynamics overview, and it never says there is a second perspective.** `/` opens on Dynamics with three boxes labelled "11 feedback loops inside" and "36 feedback loops inside" (`ceb-tf-home.png`). Nothing says that "Blocks" is the same model drawn as control loops, even though that is the central idea. The clearest statement of the idea in the whole application is Guide frame 2 (`ceb-guide-1.png`): the same loop drawn as stock and flow on the left and as a block diagram on the right, under the sentence "System dynamics draws what is; control draws what we control." A newcomer reaches that frame only by finding the Guide.
2. **The control perspective's first screen contains no control loop.** At the top level of Blocks I see opaque `Q` boxes. ticket-factory has three, joined by one unlabelled wire (`ceb-tf-blocks.png`). big has seven in a column with no wires, no r and no y (`ceb-big-blocks-dark.png`). There is no r, no Σ and no e. A control engineer's first look at "what we control" shows nothing they recognise as control.
3. **The headline is always the tool's own upkeep, and it is always off.** On every demo the first two items in Needs attention are `requests-handled` and `model-fits`, both off 87–100% of the time (`ceb-tf-home.png`, `ceb-sf-blocks.png`). The first thing the product teaches is "ergostat's own loops do not hold".
4. **The control analysis it is proud of never appears.** `/model` on every demo says "an integrator, from the model … its dead time: a step of u, or a step test", or "a best guess, not proposed". Not one loop in six demos has an identified process, so no panel ever shows margins, a closed-loop step, a Bode plot or a SIMC tuning (`ceb-is-ctrl-panel.png`: "Its process is not identified yet, so no margins, closed-loop step or tuning"). Those are exactly the features that would convince an SRE or a control engineer.
5. **"On top of existing tools" is never shown.** No demo includes an observed external controller, such as an autoscaler or an alert rule identified as C(s). Yet WHY.md's strongest argument rests on that ("an autoscaler that oscillates, lags its load, sits pinned at its maximum…").

**What would have made it obvious, within the decisions** (no new pages or panels, two separate diagrams, recognised types only):

- Open the Diagram page on the split it already has (Dynamics | Blocks, `DESIGN.md` §6 "Two views at once"), with "Colour by correspondence" turned on, for the system that needs attention. Guide frame 2 already does exactly this, so it only needs to become the default view. Each diagram stays pure; the shared colours teach "one model, two readings" without a word.
- Draw the top level of Blocks as the textbook loop one level up (see change B and `ceb-annotated-sf-top.png`).
- Set the `modelling` system apart, and rank the plant's loops first (change E).
- Ship one demo with an identified process and one with an observed external controller (change G).

## 3. Where the UI contradicts the intent, and what to keep

**Contradictions**

- **"The control diagram is the main view"** (DESIGN.md §6; the README says the page "opens on the control diagram"), but the page opens on Dynamics. The README's description of that "control diagram" (tanks, pipes, bow-tie valves, gauges, controllers with Σ) is the old mixed notation that decision 2 forbids. The documents and the UI disagree about which view comes first and what it looks like.
- **"A function in every block"** (decision 2), yet many blocks hold none: `fixer`, `judge`, `api-q0-drain` and `improver` are drawn as name-only boxes, the panel says "u = f(its inputs) … not a transfer function", and "check, read for requests" is a sensor with no H (`ceb-tf-modelling-blocks-fit.png`, `ceb-big-api-blocks.png`). Some of these laws are known: big's README says the drain "moves its queue down to half its goal".
- **"Instantly clear to an expert of its discipline"**: the large Blocks layouts fail this test. Chains run bottom-up (big/api), and loops are interleaved rather than one per row (ticket-factory/modelling, with its plant P_unanswered-requests at the bottom left under the controller that drives it).
- **"Systems are namespaced; shown by short name"**: "Q_service service" shows both.
- **Large systems matter** (decision 6): the big demo's control view gives no overview at the top and an unreadable Fit once opened.
- **Simplest UI, the diagram represents almost everything**: the controller and process panels are long prose (the step-test paragraph is about 250 words), and the loop's health lives on /model and /check rather than on the diagram.

**What it does well (keep all of it)**

- The Guide's control frames are textbook Åström and Murray: r, Σ with signs, e, C with K_p, P(s), H on the feedback path, y_m (`ceb-guide-1.png`, `ceb-guide-11.png`). Frame 2's side-by-side is the best explanation of the product.
- A discrete controller is drawn the way I would draw it: sampler T, dead band drawn as a dead zone before C(z) = 350z/(z−1), ZOH, a summing junction where a second command (u_adopted) joins, and a MIMO mux into P_service(s) 4×4 (`ceb-is-service-into.png`).
- Selecting by correspondence: clicking C_tuner lights up the sampler, C and ZOH together, because they are one part (`ceb-is-ctrl-panel.png`).
- The controller panel: scope (y with r dashed, u as stems, e), off periods shaded, time off, back-in time, settling, overshoot, saturation, "left out: step tests and starts", and an honest "Harris index needs the loop's dead time".
- The process panel: the MIMO transfer matrix with **structural zeros and path signs**, K ± σ, θ ± σ, Ljung–Box, cross-correlation with u, a closed-loop data caveat, a refusal to propose an unidentified model, and a sized step test with abort limits (`ceb-is-process-panel.png`). This is better practice than most commercial identification tools show.
- The serial coupling in big/api is modelled correctly: u_api-q0-out enters P_api-q0's junction with − and P_api-q1's with + (`ceb-big-api-blocks.png`). Only the layout is wrong.
- Port pills at a subsystem edge ("from rollout", "to lab"), vector slashes with widths, and the "Design SVG" export.
- Dark mode is clean on every diagram I opened.

## 4. Significant changes I would make

**A. Open on the two perspectives together.** The default Diagram is the existing split: Dynamics on the left and Blocks on the right, both drawn for the system that most needs attention, with Colour by correspondence on. Sketch: on big/api, the left pane shows the four tanks in a chain with B8–B11. The right pane shows the four loops, one per row. api-q1 in the left pane and P_api-q1(s) with y_api-q1 in the right pane share one muted hue, and pointing at either rings the other. No new UI is needed: the split, the shared selection and the correspondence colours all exist. Only the default changes. At phone width, where there is no split, open on Blocks fitted to the off loop.

**B. Make the top level of Blocks the textbook loop, one level up.** See `ceb-annotated-sf-top.png`. For software-factory: r = [no-live-bugs, few-missing, users-growing] enters a Σ, e goes to **K_factory** (it acts, so it is the controller), u_deployed goes to **G_product(s)**, y_product (3 wide) returns through H. Classify each subsystem by what it holds: a controller subsystem is K (or C), a plant-only subsystem is G or P and keeps the same letter when opened, and only a genuinely mixed subsystem stays a plain named subsystem block (Simulink's convention, with the name only). Drop Q, which my field reads as the Youla parameter. Draw uncoupled subsystems, as in big and the-job, as a block-diagonal row or grid labelled "no signals between these" rather than a bare column.

**C. Lay large Blocks out in signal-flow order, one loop per row, and fold rather than shrink.** A chain of loops in series reads as the textbook "tanks in series": q0's loop on the first row, q1's below and to the right, with the coupling u_q0,out → +Σ of P_q1 running down. Upstream comes first and goes top to bottom, never bottom-up. Fit must contain every wire. When a minimum text size (9px, already in DESIGN.md) cannot be kept, Fit should show the rows that fit and let the reader step through rows (the "wrap loops into rows" mechanism) instead of shrinking to 4px. Opening a subsystem in place must bring it into view.

**D. Draw a one-sided goal as what it is.** A ceiling is not a setpoint. Draw it with recognised blocks: r̄ into Σ, then a **one-sided dead zone** (e⁺ = min(0, r̄ − y)) before C. The diagram then explains on its own why the error is 0 within bound and why "past its bound" differs from "time off". Use the same words everywhere: *limit* (≤ or ≥) versus *setpoint* (= ± tolerance), and *limit violation* versus *error*. In Dynamics, keep Sterman's "desired" and "gap/excess", which is right for that discipline, but stop calling it "setpoint" in shared panels.

**E. Separate the model's upkeep from the system's loops.** Keep `modelling` as a system (it is a good idea: the model's quality as a stock with its own loop), but draw it in both views below a rule labelled "the model's upkeep". Rank it after the plant's loops in Needs attention and in "N loops off". Collapse identical requests into one row ("24 × holds no-unit on stock.*-q*, Deny all"). Then make it hold in the demos. Today it is off on all six, so the first lesson the product teaches is that its own feedback fails.

**F. Put a function in every block, and the loop's health on its blocks.** A program actuator with a known law gets it written in, e.g. `u_k = max(0, y_k − r̄/2)` for the drains. One with an unknown law is drawn as the recognised static nonlinearity N(·) with "law not stated", not as a name-only box. A sensor that counts items gets H = 1 with its period like every other sensor. Model health goes onto the plant block as text under P, in the style it already uses for "not identified": "balance off by 30.5 (−49%)", or "R² 0.00". The actuator gets "commanded 18×, moved 0" under its block when that is the case, so finding 1's contradiction cannot arise. The same numbers then appear in the panel and the suggestion.

**G. Demos that show the claims.** (1) One demo whose loop has an **identified process**, with a step test already run, so the controller panel shows margins, a closed-loop step, Bode and a SIMC suggestion. Without it the best control analysis in the product is invisible. (2) One demo with an **observed external controller**, such as a Kubernetes HPA identified as C(s) with its saturation and lag shown, plus a proposed retune and its simulated effect. That is WHY.md's main argument ("fixing the controls we already pay for"), and the owner's memory mentions a kube-sim demo ready for it. This is demo work, not new UI.

**H. Make the identification and controller panels tables first, prose last.** Keep every number, but lead with a 2×3 table: estimation/validation × NRMSE fit (defined), residual whiteness, independence from u. Then K, τ, θ ± σ. Then one line of verdict. Move the 250-word step-test paragraph behind "how it is sized". Fix the scoping and garbling (finding 11).

**I. Export the model from the existing Export menu.** Add "Model (XMILE)" for the Dynamics view, already listed in DISCIPLINES.md, and "Loops (JSON / .m)" for Blocks: per loop r, C(z) or law, P(s) with ±σ, H, sample periods, and the window's r, y_m, u, e traces as CSV. This serves the design-document and projection value WHY.md leads with, without adding a page.

**J. Name the two kinds of loop differently.** Use "feedback loops (R/B)" in Dynamics and "control loops" in Blocks and on the Loops page. Make a parent's count the de-duplicated union of its children's, and say so.
